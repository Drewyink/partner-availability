The challenge

  • Customers call or email to check stock, or work from a stale spreadsheet.
  • Kits and conversions make the same unit look available in more than one place.
  • Distributors and resellers need different levels of detail.
  • A wrong number becomes a backorder and a lost order.

The outcome

  • One availability definition, signed off by operations, IT and accounting.
  • Self-service stock checks without a phone call.
  • No double promises, enforced by soft reservations.
  • Controlled visibility per customer and user type.
  • Fill rate and feed health reporting for the internal team.

The approach in four layers

LayerWhat it does
1. Source of truthThe ERP and warehouse system stay the record for on hand, reservations, holds and bills of materials. Nothing customer-facing reads them directly.
2. AvailabilityOne calculated measure: on hand minus reservations, holds, soft reservations and a safety buffer. Kits and conversions are calculated from one shared parts pool.
3. RulesA single rules table decides who sees what. Distributors see exact quantities, resellers see stock bands, no one sees another customer's orders.
4. OutletsThe same number goes out as an EDI 846 feed, a partner portal and internal reporting. Every order, by EDI or portal, takes a soft reservation the moment it arrives.

Guiding principles

  • Extend the ERP; never keep a second copy of inventory.
  • One definition of available, used by every channel.
  • Kits never carry their own stock number.
  • Visibility rules are data, not code.
  • Every rule has a test before a partner connects.

Before the first partner connects

  • BOMs complete and checked for every kit and converted model.
  • Safety buffers and reseller bands approved by operations and sales.
  • EDI or portal channel agreed with each distributor.
  • All control tests passing on the Tests tab.
  • Pilot with NorthStar and one reseller before full rollout.
